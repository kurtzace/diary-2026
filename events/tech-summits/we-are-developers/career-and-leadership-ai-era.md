## Overview

[1523] "The Last Generation to Learn It the Hard Way: What Comes Next for Engineering in India" —
panel, Wed 25 Nov, 15:30-16:00, Mainstage. Panelists: Bharat Sharma, Wolfgang Platz, Aditya Dua,
Prerit Munjal. Teaser: "For twenty years, the advantage here was scale and execution — more
engineers, delivering more, faster. Both are now cheap. This panel asks what developers in India
build their careers on instead."

The thesis is blunt: the Indian IT-services value proposition for two decades has been
*headcount × delivery speed*. AI coding agents are compressing both — a small team with agents
can now out-deliver a large offshore team on routine work — so the panel's real question is what
differentiates an Indian engineer's career when "more hands, faster shipping" stops being scarce.

## Themes/vocabulary to listen for

- "Scale and execution" as a commoditized value prop vs. judgment, architecture, and taste as the
  scarce ones
- Shift from IC-as-headcount to IC-as-multiplier (one senior engineer directing several agents)
- Where "last generation to learn it the hard way" cuts both ways: hard-won debugging/production
  instinct as a moat vs. as a skill nobody will need to relearn
- Entrepreneurship/solopreneurship as an explicit alternative career track to climbing a services org

## Connects to your work

You've already made one version of this transition once — from Infosys offshore delivery
(ANZ, NAB, Westpac, Bank of America, Visa, Commonwealth Bank) into a Technical
Expert/Architect role at Société Générale, and now into an AI-tooling-building role at Cisco
(FastMCP onboarding agent, Bedrock prompt management, AI code review). This panel is
effectively asking what the *next* repositioning looks like for someone exactly in your position.

Questions worth bringing in:
1. You moved from "ship features" to "own architecture" once already — is the next move
   "own agent/tool platforms for your org" (what you're already doing) or something more
   external/entrepreneurial (Platz's solopreneur framing)?
2. If multi-agent orchestration (Dua's framing) becomes the default SDLC, does 17 years of
   full-stack breadth (.NET, Java, React, Python, Android) become more or less valuable than deep
   specialization in one layer?
3. Sharma's claim is the bottleneck moved from code to "everything around it" — does that match
   what you've seen at Cisco, where your own hackathon/prototype work (bug investigation,
   compliance assistant) was aimed at exactly those surrounding processes, not raw code output?
