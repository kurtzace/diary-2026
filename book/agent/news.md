## Models Ready but Data Isn't: Google's Gutmans on AI's Next Big Hurdle
Google’s Amit Gutmans highlights that data availability, rather than AI model capability, is the main constraint for corporate adoption. Global multinational clients value engineering talent but face hurdles because their data is fragmented across different formats and silos, which restricts real-time operations and slows down local engineering conversion.
## Vertex Eyes RapidCanvas and Supernova Funding to Sharpen AI Play: Sources
Vertex Ventures is leading investment rounds to inject $20–30 million into RapidCanvas and up to $20 million into edtech firm Supernova. RapidCanvas offers an AI-powered platform for business users, while Supernova provides AI tutoring services. The deals aim to capitalize on growing corporate and educational AI demand.
## Meta Expands its Muse AI in Small Biz Push
Meta is rolling out its AI assistant, Muse, to small businesses across India to automate customer interactions. The tool helps merchants handle FAQs, manage product catalogs, and coordinate sales directly within WhatsApp and Instagram Messenger. This expansion focuses on boosting small business efficiency and driving digital trade.
## No AI Safety without Global Collaboration: Nasscom Chair
Nasscom Chair Srikanth Velamakanni stresses that achieving AI safety requires global open-source cooperation and data-sharing. He warns against fragmented regulations, noting that while AI performance is doubling rapidly, effective safety standards cannot be achieved by any single nation. He urges a unified approach to mitigate systemic risks.
## AI Automation Startup EliseAI Valued at $4b in Latest Fundraise
Real estate automation platform EliseAI achieved a $4 billion valuation after its latest funding round. The startup utilizes conversational AI to automate property management tasks, including tenant communication, maintenance requests, and leasing schedules. The new capital will be used to expand its services into the healthcare sector.
## Owning the Full Stack: Multi-billion Dollar Global Battle for AI Control
Tech giants are locked in a multi-billion dollar race to control both AI software and hardware silicon. Companies like Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are heavily investing in custom chips to reduce dependence on Nvidia. This full-stack control is seen as vital for lowering computing costs and dominating the AI market.
## Insiders Warn That We Are Losing Grip on Self-improving AI Systems
AI researchers warn that rapid progress toward self-improving AI systems is outpacing safety guardrails. Prominent industry figures caution that as models begin training other models independently, human oversight becomes increasingly difficult. They urge stricter safety protocols before these systems develop irreversible, autonomous capabilities.
