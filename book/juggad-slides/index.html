Jugaad Innovation

Frugal, Flexible & Fast Innovation

By Navi Radjou, Jaydeep Prabhu & Sumona Ahuja

Stanford Design for Extreme Affordability

What is Jugaad?

A Hindi word meaning "frugal, flexible, and fast innovation"

An innovative fix or hack made in limited resources

Turning constraints into opportunities

Jugaad is about doing more with less, thinking creatively under pressure, and delivering value even when resources are scarce.

The 6 Principles of Jugaad

  1. Seek Opportunity in Adversity
  2. Do More with Less
  3. Think and Act Flexibly
  4. Keep it Simple
  5. Include the Margin
  6. Follow Your Heart

1. Seek Opportunity in Adversity

Converting challenges into catalysts for innovation

Challenge to Acceleration

Tulsi Tanti & Suzlon Energy

Textile business faced severe energy issues → Created wind energy solutions

Impact: Pioneering renewable energy in India

More Examples

OP Bhat (SBI)

Launched SMS-based customer complaint system

Democratized banking customer service

Junco Mexico

Free solar panel installation, cost recovered over 10 years through energy savings

Made sustainability economically viable

Mindset Shift

  • Don't retreat into a shell during adversity
  • Embrace infrastructure challenges as opportunities
  • Build psychological capital: confidence, resilience, hope
  • Approach with growth mindset, not fixed mindset

3. Think & Act Flexibly

Adaptability and improvisation as core competencies

Telemedicine Innovation

Dr. Mohan (Telemedicine Pioneer)

Partnered with ISRO satellites instead of expensive equipment

Trained local youth for equipment operation

Rural healthcare access without heavy capex

Flexibility in Action

Tata Nano

Adapted location from West Bengal to Gujarat after farmer protests

Removed executive intimidation from showroom

Improvised washing machine when farmers used it unexpectedly

Successful pivot to affordability leader

Speed, Agility & Decision Making

  • Small management units - Independent, autonomous decisions
  • Keep improvising - Like a jazz orchestra, not rigid processes
  • Fast-fail culture - Google, HTC, Huawei launch "good enough" products in weeks
  • Experiment openly - Google's 70/20/10 model led to Gmail & Maps

Break Constraints When Needed

  • Don't stick to winning formulas (risk aversion → obsolescence)
  • Kodak rejected digital; Amazon reinvented PC business
  • IBM abandoned PC business, embraced Smart Planet
  • Fail cheap, fail fast, fail often

Organizational Flexibility

  • Partner with flexible thinkers
  • Break organizational silos for speed
  • Dismantle rigid development timelines
  • Flatten hierarchy for faster innovation
  • Create cross-functional teams (design, engineering, marketing)

4. Keep It Simple

Elegant solutions without unnecessary complexity

Obstacles to Simplicity

  • Disengaged employees fearing cannibalization
  • Rigid development timelines
  • Inflexible investors/customers ("faster horses")
  • Market research limiting radical innovation
  • Belief that customers pay for more features

Simple Innovation Examples

Dr. Sarya Jeganathan - Simple Incubator

100W bulb, local wood, plexiglass = $5 incubator

Easier maintenance, affordable, wider reach

Solar Bottle Bulb

Bleach-treated water + sun refraction = 55-watt equivalent lighting

Free sustainable lighting for developing regions

Simple Design Principles

Philips "Sense and Simplicity"

No PowerPoint >10 slides, all R&D for simplicity

TV horizontal removal, daily use vacuum, simple TV design

Facebook Naming

Photos, Groups, Message, Posts - simple words for features

30% reduction in home networking product returns

Real-World Simple Solutions

  • Ushahidi: SMS-based relief coordination (Kenya, Washington Post)
  • Aakash Tablet: 3K INR tablet with WiFi, solar, Android
  • SELCO Solar Lantern: Affordable renewable lighting
  • OXO Products: One-hand salad spinner, no-bend measuring
  • Google Search: Simple not simplistic

Key Principles

  • Build ground-up simple (not stripped-down complex)
  • Humanize technology for all ages
  • Design from need, not desire
  • Backlash against complexity is real
  • 50% of support costs are from complexity

5. Include the Margin

Serve underserved populations as primary market

The Margin is Becoming the Majority

  • 25% of Europeans will be >65 years old
  • Senior market = 45% of total family spending
  • Growing Hispanic population in US
  • Middle class shrinking in developed nations
  • Long tail becoming the fat tail (long tail economics)

Inclusive Business Strategy

  • Maslow's Hierarchy: Start with basic needs (food, safety)
  • KidZania: Affordable experiential learning vs. Disney's 6L parks
  • GE Performance Contracts: Pay-per-use vs. capex investment
  • Brand Portfolios: Create value chains for different segments
  • Partner with NGOs: YES Bank + microfinance distribution

Policy & Systemic Change

  • C-level buy-in required for inclusion at scale
  • Systematic business model changes
  • Telemetry & inclusive app design
  • Performance-based healthcare reimbursement (Medicare example)
  • Inclusive work culture (include janitors in strategy)

Integrating Jugaad Into Organizations

Making innovation systemic and scalable

When to Use Jugaad vs. Traditional

Best for Jugaad:

Rapid changes, resource scarcity, diverse customers, industry maturity, interconnectivity

Best for Traditional:

Volume-oriented, hard capital infusion, efficiency focus

Jugaad is a screwdriver; traditional is the hammer

Scale Model: GE in India

  • FDG Production: Imported FDG → Local PET production delivered in hours
  • MAC 400: Portable ECG at 1/10th Western cost
  • V-Scan: Portable ultrasound with extended battery life
  • Pay-per-Scan Model: Hospitals afford diagnostics
  • Then Scale with Six Sigma for consistency

Implementation Roadmap

  1. Go for low-hanging fruit first
  2. Active top-down support
  3. Driven from bottom-up
  4. Celebrate jugaad innovators
  5. Create urgency & societal challenges
  6. Monetize (don't just patent)
  7. Create innovation brokering functions
  8. Cross-pollinate ideas
  9. Scale with Six Sigma after proving concept

Organizational Requirements

  • Rapid changes or white specs
  • Resource scarcity acceptance
  • Diverse customer base
  • Industry maturity or disruption
  • Interconnected ecosystem
  • Flat hierarchy & cross-functional teams
